# Forecast calibration (M4)

A forecast that is off gives the agent wrong information. This report checks every forecast the
simulator offers against what actually happens (spec §8.1, issue #130).

## Setup

- **Bundle**: Cambridge 2024. Dockless, 800 bikes, no trucks.
- **Windows**: every 20 min from 06:00 to 22:00, the next **30 min** are forecast for each of the **91
  station catchments**. A node belongs to the catchment of the station whose demand spreads to it most.
- **Demand**:
  - `poisson-sep`: generated by the model, 7 days from Mon 2024-09-16, 5 seeds (1,680 windows).
  - `replay-sep`: the recorded trips of that week replayed as requests, 3 seeds (1,008 windows). This is
    real data, so the model is not the truth.
  - `replay-dec`: the recorded trips of Mon 2024-12-02 to Sun 12-08, 3 seeds. This is outside the fit
    period (Apr–Nov), with the strongest within-month trend of the year.
- **Realised values** come from the event log. A trip is in progress at `t` if it started riding by `t`.
- **Errors**:
  - bias of the city-wide total per window, with its standard error over (seed, day) blocks, because the
    windows of a day share its demand level;
  - mean absolute error summed over the 91 groups;
  - for the sampled forecast (40 scenarios), the share of realised group counts inside the central
    50 % / 90 % intervals (randomised PIT, as counts are discrete) and the PIT histogram of the
    city-wide total.

Reproduce: `python scripts/validate_forecast.py <bundle> [--generator replay] [--start ...] --days 7 --seeds 5`.
`tests/sim/test_forecast_calibration.py` runs the same check in CI on a synthetic bundle.

## Findings

1. **With the model's own demand every level is calibrated.**
   - `origin` (the default): +0.03 ± 0.05 arrivals per window, out of 60.5.
   - `count`: −0.3 %.
   - `destination`: exact by construction.
   - The sampled intervals cover 49.1 % and 89.0 % (nominal 50 % / 90 %), and the PIT of the total is flat.
2. **On real (replayed) trips, arrivals in progress are slightly under-predicted**: −1.7 % with
   `origin`, −3 to −4 % with `count`. Coverage stays near nominal (48.6–49.0 %, 88.1–88.6 %), but the
   PIT of the total leans high.
   - Trips from outside are exact. Trips from the city are about 4 % low, and the gap grows with the
     trip's age (30–60 min: 126 predicted vs 165 realised).
   - Given a trip still running after 30 min, the fitted model gives too much weight to far
     destinations (strongly smoothed destination distribution) and to slow speeds. Real long trips
     tend to end soon.
   - Tracked in **#143** (learn the conditional completion from recorded trips, M7).
3. **Knowing the destination is worth far more than knowing the origin.**
   - Error summed over the 91 groups per window: about 50 for `none`, `count` and `origin` alike, and
     0 for `destination`.
   - Per group, the uncertainty is where a trip goes, not how many are in progress. The origin mainly
     fixes the total and its timing.
   - This is the measured cost of not knowing destinations (D15).
4. **Departures need the current demand level.** The model has one level per month. Without
   adaptation, the December week was predicted at **−48 %**, because the first week of December runs
   at about 4,500 trips/day against a December mean of 2,891. Each day also has its own level (±22 %).
   The forecast now estimates the level from the requests of the last 6 h (Gamma prior of 50
   requests):
   - departures are within +0.3 % (simulated), +0.7 % (September replay) and −3.9 % (December replay,
     where the level lags a fast trend);
   - the demand model itself should get a smooth seasonal level: **#144**.
5. **Only served requests become arrivals, so what is observed matters.** Three corrections brought
   new arrivals from +15 % to +1.0 % (simulated) and −0.3 % (September replay); December stays at
   −8 %:
   - the share of requests that get a bike, **per origin place** and over the **recent** window
     (failures cluster where and when bikes are short);
   - the same share for requests **from outside**, because the reservoir empties as a run goes on;
   - the time users walk to their bike.
6. **A simulator bug was found and fixed on the way (#138).** The ride-time model's lowest speed
   quantile is 0 m/s, which produced endless rides (0.14 % over 3 h, up to 950 days). Bikes in use
   accumulated, and `count` was biased by +3 %; it is unbiased after the cap.
